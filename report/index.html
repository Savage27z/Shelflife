How many NEAR contracts actually match their published source?

A blockchain runs machine code. Projects publish readable source and say “this is what it does.” Almost nobody checks whether the two match. This measures it.

Metadata presence overstates verifiability. 19 of the 53 contracts exposing metadata publish no link in it — a version string pointing nowhere. The honest figure is the one counting a usable link: 20.2%.
Accounts are not programs. Those 168 accounts run only 101 distinct pieces of bytecode — roughly a third are factory clones, so one factory’s choice can swing the aggregate. Counting distinct programs instead: of 87 unique-bytecode contracts, 41.4% expose metadata.

What this does and does not claim

Verification coverage, not honesty. A contract publishing no source is unverifiable — that is not evidence anyone is lying, and no project here is accused of anything. The fix is two commands.

Nothing has been rebuilt. Every figure measures whether a source link is published, not whether that source compiles to the deployed bytes. Read “verifiable” as “a link exists to check”, never as “checked”.

Findings 1 and 2 are complete, enumerating the whole registry. Findings 3 and 4 are a sample of token contracts, and token contracts may not behave like the rest of the chain.
